Excel and Google Sheets are brilliant. Almost every business I work with started on one, and for a long time that was the right call: free, flexible, and everyone already knows how to use it. The problem is that spreadsheets grow quietly. One day the sheet that tracked ten orders is tracking hundreds, three people edit it at once, and nobody is quite sure which version is correct.

Outgrowing a spreadsheet does not automatically mean you need custom software. Sometimes a cleaner sheet is the answer. But if you recognise yourself in the signs below, it is worth thinking about what comes next.

## 1. One person is the only one who understands the sheet {#sign-1}

There is a tab, a formula or a colour code that only one person really understands. When they are on leave, things slow down or go wrong. That is not a spreadsheet anymore. It is a single point of failure for your business.

## 2. You type the same information in more than one place {#sign-2}

An order comes in on WhatsApp, gets typed into a sheet, then into an invoice, then into a stock list. Every copy is a chance for a typo, and every typo turns into a missed delivery, a wrong bill or an awkward phone call. If the same customer or item lives in three places, your data is already drifting apart.

## 3. Nobody can answer "how are we doing?" without a phone call {#sign-3}

How much stock is left? Which leads are waiting for a reply? What did we sell this week? If answering takes someone opening four files and adding things up, you are flying blind most of the time. Good systems answer these questions on a screen, any time, on any phone.

## 4. Mistakes are found by customers, not by you {#sign-4}

Double-booked rooms. An item sold that was not in stock. A follow-up that never happened. Spreadsheets do not warn you when something is wrong, so problems surface when a customer complains. A proper system can stop the mistake from happening in the first place: no double bookings, alerts before stock runs out, a reminder when a lead goes quiet.

## 5. You are scared to touch it {#sign-5}

The sheet is slow, it breaks when someone sorts the wrong column, and there is a copy called "FINAL v3 (use this one)". If the team is nervous about editing the thing that runs the business, it is time to change something.

## What to do next: three honest options {#what-next}

### Option 1: Fix the spreadsheet

If you only recognise one or two signs, a cleanup may be enough. Lock the formula columns, use dropdowns instead of free typing, split one giant sheet into clear tabs, and give each person a defined job in it. This costs very little and buys time.

### Option 2: Move to an off-the-shelf tool

For common jobs like accounting, invoicing, appointment booking or a simple CRM, there are good ready-made tools. If one fits how you work with small compromises, use it. It is usually the fastest and cheapest route, and I will tell you so.

### Option 3: Build a system around your process

Custom software makes sense when your process is what makes you different, or when you are paying for several tools that still do not talk to each other. A focused web app can take the work your team does in sheets and chats and put it in one place: stock across locations, orders, leads, bookings, with the right people seeing the right screens and the numbers always up to date.

> **Start small**
>
> The best first version is not everything. Pick the one sheet that causes the most pain, replace that, and let the team use it for real before adding more.

## What a proper system gives you that a sheet cannot {#what-good-looks-like}

- One source of truth. Every customer, order or item lives in one place, so there is no "which file is right?"
- The right access for each person. Staff see and change only what they need; the owner sees everything.
- A history of changes. You can see who changed what and when, instead of guessing.
- Rules that prevent mistakes. A room cannot be booked twice; an order cannot go out for stock you do not have.
- Alerts. Low stock, an overdue payment or a lead nobody has called back shows up before it becomes a problem.
- It works on a phone. Staff on the floor, in the kitchen or on the road can update things as they happen.
- Reports without the copy-paste. This week's numbers are a screen away, not an afternoon of adding up tabs.

## Common worries, answered {#common-worries}

### "My team is used to Excel. Will they actually use something new?"

They will if it is simpler than what they do today. That is the bar. A good system is built around the steps your team already follows, uses their words for things, and removes work rather than adding it. Involving one or two people from the team in the early versions helps more than any training session.

### "What happens to all our old data?"

It comes with you. Moving the existing sheets into the new system is planned from the start, including cleaning up duplicates and odd formats along the way. You should never have to start from an empty screen.

### "Is this only for big companies?"

No. Small teams often feel spreadsheet pain the most, because there is no spare person to double-check everything. A focused tool for five or ten people can be small, quick to build and cheap to run.

## How to start {#how-to-start}

1. Write down the three things that go wrong most often today.
2. List who touches the sheet and what each person needs to see or change.
3. Note what must never happen: a double booking, a wrong bill, running out of a key item.

That list is a better starting point than any feature wish-list, because it describes the problem, not a solution. Bring it to a short call and we can work out together whether you need a cleaner sheet, a ready-made tool, or something built for you.
